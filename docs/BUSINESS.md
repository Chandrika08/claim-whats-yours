# Business case (draft; numbers marked ASSUMPTION must be replaced or kept clearly labelled)

## Problem
Eligible families miss benefits because information is scattered, rules are hard to read, the document list is unclear, and they pay middlemen for help that should be free.

## Who pays (families never pay)
1. NGOs and CSR programmes: pay per active family or per district per year, because they need proof of benefits unlocked. Impact dashboard is the product they buy.
2. Common service centres / village-level operators: a small monthly fee for an assisted mode that serves many families faster than paper.
3. Banks (rural branches): sponsor for financial inclusion goals (accounts, credit cards, insurance linked schemes).
4. State government: a district-level licence to publish verified scheme data and track uptake.

## Impact metrics (all with visible assumptions)
- Families reached
- Average schemes matched per family
- Estimated annual benefit unlocked = matched schemes x average benefit value (each scheme's benefit value from its official page)
- Middleman fees avoided (ASSUMPTION)

## Why it stays accurate
Rules are editable data with an official source and last verified date. Over 90 days old shows a re-verification badge. A named verifier per state updates data. Users can flag a wrong rule (future).

## Rollout
Pilot in one district with one NGO partner and 5 to 10 service centres, measure applications started and completed, then extend to the next group (students, senior citizens).

## Risks and answers
- Rules change: editable data + stale badge.
- Wrong advice: "likely eligible" wording, official links, confirm with the office.
- Low literacy: voice input and local language (roadmap), assisted mode through operators.
